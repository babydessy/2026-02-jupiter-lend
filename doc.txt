# Interacting with the Lending Protocol

Fluid’s lending interface is designed for simplicity, making it easy to deposit your assets and start earning yield from both lending and fluid rewards.

## What is Lending on Fluid?

When you deposit your tokens (like USDC, ETH, or USDT), they get supplied into Fluid’s lending pools. These tokens can then be borrowed by other users. In return, you earn -

* **Lending APR** - interest from borrowers
* **Rewards APR** - bonus rewards from the protocol
* Combined, this gives you your **Net APR**

## How to Start Lending

1. Connect your wallet (EOA, DSA, or Avocado)
2. Go to the [**Lend**](https://fluid.io/lending/1) tab on the Fluid dashboard
3. Choose the token you want to deposit (e.g, USDC or ETH)
4. Click **Start Earning**
5. Enter the amount to deposit
6. Approve the token if it’s your first time
7. Confirm the deposit transaction in your wallet

Built on top of Fluid's modular Liquidity Layer, these vaults offer -

* **Highest LTVs**(Loan to value)**,** borrow up to 95% of your collateral’s value
* **Better rates** for both lenders and borrowers
* Ultra-low **liquidation penalty** (as low as 0.1%)
* **Automated ceilings** to prevent risky whale movements
* **Efficient liquidations** with 3–4× lower gas costs
* **Maximum capital efficiency** through unified liquidity across protocols

## Close Your Vault <a href="#close-your-vault" id="close-your-vault"></a>

To close your vault, you can use either of these two options:

* **Payback** – Repay your full debt from your wallet. After that, you can **withdraw your collateral** (the same asset you deposited).
* **Deleverage** – Reduce your position by using part of your collateral to repay the debt. In this case, you’ll receive your **remaining collateral in the form of the debt token**.

Your vault is considered closed once both your **debt is repaid** and your **collateral is withdrawn**.



# Net APY

## What is Net APY? <a href="#what-is-net-apy" id="what-is-net-apy"></a>

Net APY (Annual Percentage Yield) tells you the effective yield you're earning on your position after accounting for both:

* Earnings from your supplied assets
* Interest paid on your borrowed assets

It represents your actual return on your net capital (what you own after borrowing), annualized at current rates.

#### Why Does Net APY Matter? <a href="#why-does-net-apy-matter" id="why-does-net-apy-matter"></a>

* Shows your true performance in a leverage vault
* Helps you compare whether a strategy is worth it after borrow costs
* Influences your decision to loop more, deleverage, or exit

### How is Net APY Calculated? <a href="#how-is-net-apy-calculated" id="how-is-net-apy-calculated"></a>

**Formula -**

$$
Net APY=

(Total Collateral× Net~~Collateral APR)−(Total Debt× Net~~Debt APR)
​/ (Total Collateral - Total Debt)
$$

#### In simpler terms:

1. **Calculate earnings** on Collateral assets.
2. **Subtract the cost** of your Debt assets.
3. Divide that difference by your **net position** (Collateral - Debt).

## Real Example

Lets take the following USDe-USDT/USDC-USDT position which is earning you 98.71% Net APY.

<figure><img src="https://1779047404-files.gitbook.io/~/files/v0/b/gitbook-x-prod.appspot.com/o/spaces%2F1GnplQv2H5lIIg0ygng0%2Fuploads%2F8tdD89gD68NQ4IfWqyn3%2Fimage45.png?alt=media&#x26;token=9548102c-1edc-4d48-bbb1-97c233946301" alt=""><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

Step-by-step Calculation:

$$
Net APY=

(11,269,893×11.18%)−(10,553,663×5.24%)
​/ (11,269,893−10,553,663)
$$

1. **Earning:**

$$
11,269,893×11.18%=1,259,870.50
$$

2. **Cost of Borrowing:**

$$
10,553,663×5.24%=552,996.94
$$

3. **Net Position:**

$$
11,269,893−10,553,663=716,230
$$

4. **Net APY:**

$$
(1,259,870.50−552,996.94) / 716,230 =98.71%
$$

This confirms that the position is earning a positive return after accounting for borrowing costs.

#### Tips for Interpreting Net APY

**High Net APY (Positive) -** \
You’re earning significantly more than you’re paying in interest, the position is performing well.

**Low Net APY (Positive) -**\
You’re earning slightly more than you’re paying. The return is modest, consider reviewing or optimizing your strategy.

**Negative Net APY -**\
Interest costs exceed your earnings. In simple words, you’re paying more in borrowing than you're earning from lending.

## Strategies Overview

### Compound Strategies -

These strategies do not utilize a flashloan and can be combinations of different operations.

1. **Warp < > Unwarp**&#x20;

You can convert between regular assets and their wrapped versions (e.g, ETH to wETH). This is useful when some strategies or vaults require wrapped tokens. This is just a swap, it doesn’t affect your vault position.

2. **Deposit & Borrow**

This lets you deposit your asset into the vault and borrow another asset in a single click.\
It’s useful when you want to quickly enter a borrowing position without doing two steps separately.

3. **Payback & Withdraw**

You can repay your loan and withdraw your deposited asset at the same time.\
This is helpful when you want to exit your position completely or partially in one action.

### Flashloan based Strategies -

These strategies utilize flashloans to accomplish higher capital efficiency and allow users to accomplish more in one transaction.

4. **Leverage**

* Increase your position using borrowed funds.
* This strategy uses a flashloan to borrow against your existing collateral and deposits it back, giving you more exposure to the asset.
* *Best for users who want to maximize returns but also higher risk.*

5. **Deleverage**

* Reduce your debt using your own collateral.
* It helps you unwind your position by selling part of your collateral and using that to repay debt.
* *Great when markets are risky or if you want to reduce exposure.*

# Leverage

## What is Leverage? <a href="#what-is-leverage" id="what-is-leverage"></a>

Leverage is the process of **borrowing more assets** using your deposited collateral in the vault. You’re increasing your exposure to a particular asset or position by using borrowed funds.

It’s useful when:

* You want to **maximize your exposure** to a yield-generating strategy.
* You believe the asset's value will go up and want to **amplify potential gains**.

#### **When Should You Use Leverage?**

* You’re confident in the **future value** of your collateral asset.
* You want to **maximize yield farming or staking rewards**.
* You want to **increase capital efficiency** without selling your assets.

## **How Leverage Works (Step-by-Step)** <a href="#how-leverage-works-step-by-step" id="how-leverage-works-step-by-step"></a>

**1. Borrow Against Collateral**

* You deposit collateral into a vault.
* A portion of your borrowing limit is used to borrow a debt token.

**2. Swap Debt Token into More Collateral**

* The borrowed debt token is swapped for more of the same collateral asset.

**3. Deposit Swapped Collateral Back Into Vault**

* This newly acquired collateral is deposited back into the vault.
* Your collateral increases, allowing for **more borrowing** and **higher exposure**.

## **Understanding Leverage with Examples**

> **Now that you know how leveraging works, let’s walk through some basic scenarios to understand different outcomes.**

### **Case 1: You Borrow Small – Safe Leverage**

You use a small portion of your borrowing power.

* Only a little collateral is added from leverage.
* Position remains safe with **low risk of liquidation**.
* Useful when testing or gradually increasing exposure

# Deleverage

## What is Deleverage? <a href="#what-is-deleverage" id="what-is-deleverage"></a>

Deleverage is the process of unwinding a leveraged position. If you’ve borrowed assets using your collateral (i.e. taken leverage), this strategy helps you reduce or fully repay that debt using the collateral you already have in your vault.

It's useful when -

* You want to exit your leverage position.
* You want to lower your debt to make your position safer.

**When Should You Use Deleverage?**

* You want to **close your leveraged position**
* You want to **reduce your debt** using the collateral
* You want to **improve your Debt-to-Collateral (D/C) ratio**
* You want to **safely exit** a risky position

## **How Deleverage Works (Step-by-Step)** <a href="#how-deleverage-works-step-by-step" id="how-deleverage-works-step-by-step"></a>

**1.  Withdraw Collateral**

A portion of your deposited collateral is pulled out from the vault. This is the asset you'll use to repay part (or all) of your outstanding debt.

**2.  Swap Collateral for Debt Token**

The withdrawn collateral is swapped into the token you owe (your debt token).

**3.  Repay Debt**

The swapped tokens are used to reduce or eliminate your vault’s debt.

## Understanding Deleverage with Examples

> **Now that you know what deleveraging is and why it’s useful, let’s look at a few simple examples.** \
> These will show how different collateral amounts can lead to different outcomes like, whether the debt is fully paid off, some debt remains, or excess collateral is returned.

### **Case 1: You Deleverage but Still Have Some Debt Remaining**

You deleverage partially. The position becomes safer, but some debt still remains.

* A part of the collateral is withdrawn and swapped to repay part of the debt.
* The vault still holds some remaining debt and collateral.
* Your D/C ratio improves, reducing risk.

# How a Liquidated Position Looks

Liquidation happens when your vault becomes unsafe because your collateral is not enough to cover your borrowed amount.\
To fix this, the system automatically sells part of your collateral to repay some debt and bring your vault back to a safe level.\
This protects the system and prevents you from owing more than your collateral is worth.

### How do I know if my position is liquidated?

* When a position is liquidated, you’ll see a banner/notification at the bottom of your vault.

<figure><img src="https://1779047404-files.gitbook.io/~/files/v0/b/gitbook-x-prod.appspot.com/o/spaces%2F1GnplQv2H5lIIg0ygng0%2Fuploads%2FZ9XDT4wi5F9CNcJQFukg%2Flp.png?alt=media&#x26;token=e0d6ca5e-d1b4-42d0-adcd-7e96e8b63c6f" alt=""><figcaption><p>Example of a liquidated position: part of your collateral sold to cover debt</p></figcaption></figure>

* It will show the liquidation details:
  * How much debt was repaid
  * How much collateral was withdrawn

### What happens when liquidation occurs?

* A part of your collateral is sold to repay your debt.
* This brings your position back to a **safe ratio** so it doesn’t remain at risk.
* A small liquidation penalty is also charged (visible in your vault details).

### What should I do after liquidation?

* **Review your vault** → Check the updated collateral and debt values.
* **Deposit more collateral** to reduce risk, or
* **Pay back debt** to stabilize your position further.
* Keep an eye on the **Liquidation Price** and **Ratio bar** to avoid future liquidations.

Learn more about [Vault Liquidation](https://fluid.guides.instadapp.io/vault-protocol/advanced-liquidation-mechanism/vault-liquidation) on the Vault Protocol.

# Refinance on Fluid

## What is Refinance? <a href="#what-is-refinance" id="what-is-refinance"></a>

Refinancing allows you to **migrate your current lending or borrowing position** from another supported protocol directly into Fluid. This gives you access to Fluid’s optimized rates, automation, and unified asset management, without needing to manually close and re-open positions.

* Access **Fluid’s advanced tools**, better yields, and streamlined UX
* Maintain your **better health ratio** and position size

## How Refinance Works <a href="#how-refinance-works" id="how-refinance-works"></a>

1. **Deposit**: You start with a selected amount of an asset.

6. **Final result**: You end up with a larger position and a corresponding debt - all handled automatically.

### Price Oracles

Fluid utilizes multiple price oracles, including Chainlink and Uniswap TWAP (Time Weighted Average Price) checks, to verify prices during transactions. This dual-check system minimizes the risk of price manipulation, ensuring more reliable and secure operations.

#### Chainlink Price Oracles with Uniswap TWAP Checks&#x20;

Protocols such as the Vaults protocol can utilize multiple oracles like Chainlink and Uniswap and do utilize them in combination to protect users and the protocol from bad debt.

1. Pulls on chain Uniswap TWAP price (on-chain price)
2. Checks this price against the Chainlink (difference check)
3. If the TWAP is within parameters it will use on-chain otherwise it will use the Chainlink price. (determines oracle to utilize)

In essence, this means the uniV3 price (on-chain pricing) will be utilized in most cases. However, if there is a significant market fluctuation, we will revert to using the Chainlink price. Our goal is to ensure that liquidations on the Vault protocol can occur whenever necessary, relying on the most accurate information available. This approach safeguards the protocol from incurring bad debt.

#### Contract Pricing

Certain Vaults within the Vaults protocol, such as weETH/wstETH, employ enhanced security measures through contract pricing to prevent user liquidations during short-term price depegs. By querying the backing contract for assets, we can verify that they are fully backed, ensuring accurate asset pricing even with low on-chain liquidity. This approach effectively prevents baseless liquidations due to temporary price fluctuations. Contract pricing in Fluid has some similarities to Fundamental Pricing on AAVE, providing a robust mechanism to maintain stability.

### Liquidity and Automated Limits

Fluid's innovative Automated Limits system ensures a secure and stable environment for users by continuously adjusting per block to restrict maximum withdrawals and borrowing within any 12-hour period. #### Position Parameters:

* **Liquidation Threshold -** The ratio at which the position would be considered undercollateralized and Could be liquidated.
* **Liquidation Max Limit** - The threshold above which 100% of your position gets liquidated instantly.
* **Liquidation Price** - The collateral price at which the position would be considered undercollateralized and could be liquidated.\
  Generally represented as liquidation price/current price of collateral.
* **Liquidation Penalty -** This is the penalty on your collateral asset for liquidating the position, that has crossed the liquidation threshold, just until the threshold
* **Ratio** - This is the Debt to Collateral ratio. Dollar value of Debt / Dollar value of Collateral or D/C ratio
* **Supply APR** - Rate earned per year for supplying the collateral asset
* **Borrow APR -** Rate to be paid per year for borrowing the debt asset
* **Supply Absorb -** The collateral absorbed by the protocol of a borrower who becomes under-collateralized
* **Borrow Absorb -** The borrowers debt settled during the process of collateral absorption in liquidation

#### Withdrawal Limit:

* **Base Limit** - The minimum limit for a Vault's withdrawal. The further expansion happens on this base
* **Current Limit** - The limit until where you can withdraw. If it is $0 that means 100% of the users can withdraw
* **Expand Percentage** - The rate at which Limits would increase or decrease over the given duration
* **Expand Duration** - The time for which the limits expand at the given rate
* **Withdrawable** - Amount available for instant withdrawal
* **Withdrawal Gap -** Safety non-withdrawable amount to guarantee liquidations

#### Borrow Limit:

* **Base Limit** - The minimum limit for a Vault's borrowing. The further expansion happens on this base
* **Current Limit** - The limit until where you can borrow
* **Max Limit** - Maximum limit for a vault above which it is not possible to borrow
* **Expand Percentage** - The rate at which Limits would increase or decrease over the given duration
* **Expand Duration** - The time for which the limits expand at the given rate

## Liquidations and Loan-to-Value <a href="#liquidations-and-loan-to-value" id="liquidations-and-loan-to-value"></a>

The Vault protocol recognizes that the implementation of liquidations plays a critical role in determining the Loan-to-Value (LTV) of a lending protocol. Liquidations are essential for maintaining the safety and stability of the protocol by eliminating bad debts on time. However, there are two approaches to enhancing protocol safety: reducing the LTV or improving the liquidation mechanism.

While reducing the LTV may enhance protocol safety, it also limits end-users’ borrowing capacity, giving competitors an advantage by offering better LTV ratios. On the other hand, optimizing the liquidation process can lead to a safer protocol with a higher LTV, thereby improving the overall experience and utility for end-users.

In this regard, the Vault protocol introduces a groundbreaking liquidation mechanism that represents a significant advancement in design. This mechanism has the potential to enable users to borrow up to 95% of the value of their ETH. By implementing this innovative liquidation engine, the protocol aims to strike a balance between safety and borrowing capacity, providing users with increased flexibility and maximizing the utility of their assets.

## Oracles <a href="#oracles" id="oracles"></a>

Oracles play a critical role in the functioning of lending and borrowing protocols, serving as a key component for ensuring protocol security and efficient liquidity management. ## Liquidation Mechanism <a href="#liquidation-mechanism" id="liquidation-mechanism"></a>

The liquidation mechanism at the core of the Vault protocol represents a significant leap forward, drawing inspiration from Uniswap v3 while surpassing the effectiveness of existing borrowing and lending protocols by 100x. When a user creates a position, the protocol assigns it to a specific tick based on the user’s debt-to-collateral ratio, with the tick calculated as,

*ratio = 1.0015^t, where t = tick*

This innovative approach consolidates all users’ liquidity and streamlines the liquidation process for liquidators, resembling a simple swap-like operation.

To further enhance efficiency, Fluid can be seamlessly integrated into DEX aggregators like 1inch, Paraswap, and 0x, similar to other DEX protocols like Uniswap or Curve. Such enhanced efficiency benefits borrowers, allowing them to minimize losses during liquidations effectively. Unlike other borrowing and lending protocols, which often subject users to considerable losses ranging from 50% to 100% in a single liquidation event, Fluid’s liquidation mechanism ensures that liquidation amounts are typically minimal, usually between 1% to 10%, just enough to bring the user’s position back to the liquidation threshold. By mitigating the forced selling of collateral during market crashes, Fluid’s approach prevents cascading effects and contributes to stabilizing the overall market during turbulent times.

### Ticks and Liquidity <a href="#ticks-and-liquidity" id="ticks-and-liquidity"></a>

In the Vault protocol, the allocation of a user’s position to a specific tick is determined by their debt-to-collateral ratio. This ratio, calculated as *ratio = debt/collateral*, plays a crucial role in positioning the user within the protocol. To calculate the tick, the protocol utilizes the formula *ratio = 1.0015^t*, where t = tick, represents the specific tick value associated with the user’s position.

While these calculations are conceptually straightforward, performing them at the smart contract level can be resource-intensive in terms of gas consumption.

### Branch and Liquidation <a href="#branch-and-liquidation" id="branch-and-liquidation"></a>

In Fluid, user liquidity is consolidated into ticks which ensures efficient management of positions. When a user undergoes liquidation, their position needs to be retrieved in real-time. To achieve this, Fluid employs a branch-based mechanism. A branch represents the outcome of liquidation related variables and allows users to access their recent position in a gas-optimized manner, regardless of the number of previous liquidations they have experienced.

The user’s tick prior to liquidation serves as the starting point and establishes the debt factor and connection to the branch. By iterating through the branch, starting from the user’s tick, the system identifies the minimum tick (minima) and calculates the total liquidation that has occurred. This process enables efficient and precise liquidation, even in scenarios where users have been liquidated multiple times.

The graph below visually demonstrates the movement of collateral prices and it's impact on ticks. As the collateral price rises, new users enter at higher ticks. Conversely, when the price declines, higher ticks progressively undergo liquidation and move to lower ticks.

The lifespan of a branch in Fluid extends until it reaches the minima tick of its base branch. At this stage, the branch is merged with the base branch, allowing the liquidation process to continue seamlessly from that point onward.

![Figure 2: Collateral price graph and Branch Cycle](https://1779047404-files.gitbook.io/~/files/v0/b/gitbook-x-prod.appspot.com/o/spaces%2F1GnplQv2H5lIIg0ygng0%2Fuploads%2FGSuO9kXNoaPAT1MasmWt%2F1.jpeg?alt=media)

Let’s examine the diagram above to better understand the concepts presented: With every new local maxima a new branch gets connected with the base branch. For example:

1. Branch 2 is connected with Branch 1, gets merged with Branch 1 at point *d* thus ending the lifespan of Branch 2.
2. Branch 3 is connected with Branch 1, this branch is currently an active branch. It can merge with Base Branch 1 if the collateral value falls to that level again.

Now, let’s consider the positions of users located at points *a* and *c* which represent two local maxima on the graph. Although there may be multiple users at different ticks between points *a - c* and *b - d*, for simplicity, we will concentrate on these two points.

By analyzing the diagrams, we observe that users at point *a* experience a final debt reduction of 75%, resulting in a final debt ratio of &#x30;*.*&#x32;5. The minimum tick of Branch 1 indicates the point till which their positions were liquidated.

Therefore, the final position for users at point *a* after all the liquidation can be calculated as follows:

*debt = (0.25/1) ∗ initialDebt = 0.25 ∗ initialDebt*

*ratio = 1.0015^m, where m = minimaTick*

*collateral = debt/ratio*

For users at point *c* the final debt should be &#x30;*.*&#x33;, signifying a 70% reduction in debt. In Branch 2, the debt was reduced by 40% (from 1 to &#x30;*.*&#x36;), and then the branch merged with Branch 1. Subsequently, Branch 1 underwent a liquidation of 50% (from &#x30;*.*&#x35; to &#x30;*.*&#x32;5), resulting in a total debt reduction of 1 \* &#x30;*.*&#x36; \* &#x30;*.*&#x35; = &#x30;*.*&#x33; or 30% of the initial debt. When branches merge, the debt factor of Branch 2 transitions into the connection factor.

To calculate the connection factor:

* *Debt factor of Branch 2 at merge = 0.6*
* *Debt factor of Branch 1 (base branch) at merge = 0.5*
* *Connection factor = 0.5/0.6 = 0.8333333*

To determine the final debt for users at point *c* :

*debt = \[finalDebtFactor/(initialDebt ∗ connectionFactor)] ∗ initialDebt*

*debt = (0.25/(1 ∗ 0.8333333333)) ∗ initialDebt = 0.3 ∗ initialDebt*

*ratio = 1.0015^m, where m = minimaTick*

*collateral = debt/ratio*

### Slot Based Approach

When users initiate a position by opening a vault within the protocol, their debt and collateral are calculated into a ratio, which is then assigned to a specific slot. This aligns with the range of debts held by others in similar positions. Drawing inspiration from Uniswap v3’s slot-based liquidity, this method is engineered to enhance the efficiency of liquidations.

When the value of collateral decreases to the point where the debt ratio surpasses the liquidation threshold, the protocol aggregates all vaults in that slot for liquidation in a single transaction. Afterward, the system recalibrates the remaining vaults, adjusting their debt ratio positions down to the next slot in response to the new price range.

Fluid's sophisticated liquidation means the protocol is able to efficiently remove and clear debt, this process allows for up to 95% borrowing of asset value, signifying the protocol's high capital efficiency.

### **Lower Gas Costs**

The Vault protocol dramatically lowers the gas costs associated with liquidations by streamlining the process to resemble a simple swap. This efficiency is achieved through the design of the liquidation mechanism, which allows for the simultaneous liquidation of all positions within a specific range. This approach contrasts sharply with traditional DeFi protocols, where each position must be individually targeted and liquidated, often requiring multiple transactions and, consequently, higher gas fees. By optimizing the liquidation process the protocol saves on gas but also speeds of the resolution and closure of bad debt from the protocol.

### **Minimal Impact Liquidations**

One of the key innovations of the Vault protocol is its minimal impact approach to liquidations. Unlike traditional systems where a significant portion of a user's position might be liquidated—sometimes as much as 100%—to cover bad debt, the Vault protocol only liquidates the minimum necessary to restore the account to a healthy state. This method minimizes market impact, reduces the potential for cascading liquidations (where one liquidation triggers another, causing a domino effect), and generally contributes to greater market stability. This approach is particularly effective during volatile market conditions, where aggressive liquidations can exacerbate price movements and lead to greater systemic risk.

### **Trader-as-Liquidator**

A novel aspect of the Vault protocol is its integration with DEX aggregators, enabling any trader to act as a liquidator. This model effectively turns the liquidation process into another trading opportunity, accessible through familiar platforms like 1inch, Paraswap, and 0x.&#x20;

Traders participate in the liquidation process indirectly by completing normal swaps and trades on any DEX. The portion of the collateral required to liquidate the position is made available for in the trading routes,  because liquidations are so extremely gas efficient the liquidatable collateral will have a natural discount enabling the trader to get a better swap while simultaneously liquidating debt from Fluid.  This means Fluid is less reliant on Keepers and other dedicated networks of liquidation bots and can be liquidated from normal every day users.

# ETH Borrow Rate Management

### Rate Handling for ETH&#x20;

The borrow rate of ETH is adjusted in such a way that whatever is the current wstETH lending rate(lets say x%), the borrow rate of ETH is adjusted such that at kink1 the borrow rate is (x+k)% and at kink2 the borrow rate is (x+k+0.5)% where 'k' is any constant value which can change in future.

#### For Example:

Currently the 'k' is set to 1.5% so basically if the current lending rate of wstETH is 10% then the\
-> Borrow rate of ETH at kink1 (Utilization: 50%) is (10+k)% = (10+1.5)% = 11.5%

# DEX on Fluid

## What is the DEX Protocol

The DEX protocol is a novel addition that is built on top of the Liquidity Layer, introducing new primitives in **Smart Debt** and **Smart Collateral**. This allows users to utilize their debt (for the first time) and collateral as liquidity for trading purposes.&#x20;

The DEX protocol is the single most powerful and capital efficient decentralized exchange in DeFi, offering the ability to generate up to $39 in liquidity per $1 in TVL.

<figure><img src="https://lh7-rt.googleusercontent.com/docsz/AD_4nXfZrSel1wosPcYYTBuNmy2mWHot1qG0jXbYYIu_vvO-BSskONgXO4K-93vbvitIucUUF68STTsnXMsbLL3Q86LWT15naSm-UbISqdyhGPNDiEvVJEak4G56KlBxuLto8ce1Hm7Mlg?key=Q-PYXvvJw4bkxbNNWwVn3w" alt=""><figcaption><p>A quick look at a Smart Vault position utilizing both Smart Collateral and Smart Debt. </p></figcaption></figure>

### Features of the DEX Protocol

* **Enhanced Utilization of Assets -** The DEX protocol allows for users to experiment with different strategies to create their ideal collateral/debt combinations, earning new yield on their collateral and turning debt into a productive asset.
* **Superior Capital Efficiency -** Users can create up to 39x the amount of liquidity from 1x the amount of assets, multiplying their trading fee earnings by 39 times.
* **New Financial Primitives -** Unlike traditional DEX models that use generic token LPs, Smart Collateral is tightly integrated into the Liquidity Layer, and Smart Debt represents the first case of debt being used as liquidity. This unique approach allows users to reduce their debt using the trading fees earned.




# Smart Collateral

Smart Collateral is a single range order that enables users to utilize their capital in a variety of ways including:

* **Lending it** - Giving users a simple way of earning yield with little risk or effort.
* **Borrowing against it** - This provides users with more flexibility and enables more efficient asset utilization.
* **Deploying it as AMM liquidity** - By using their collateral as automated market maker (AMM) liquidity, users can simultaneously earn both lending and trading fees. This allows for leverage, effectively increasing trading fee earnings.

In its simplest form, Smart Collateral are the DEX’s LP tokens with the added composability of being an asset that can be borrowed from and lended, whilst increasing DEX liquidity at the same time.

<figure><img src="https://1779047404-files.gitbook.io/~/files/v0/b/gitbook-x-prod.appspot.com/o/spaces%2F1GnplQv2H5lIIg0ygng0%2Fuploads%2FjYI2cWgQ9zjX4ZDhLxMM%2FSmart-Collateral-(2).gif?alt=media&#x26;token=29edb326-e2d2-4114-b55e-cb7ac1d5ed6d" alt=""><figcaption><p>Smart Collateral assets earn lending fees and benefit from swaps routed through the Fluid DEX by earning trading fees.</p></figcaption></figure>

The architecture of the DEX pool is very similar to a combination of Uniswap v2 & Uniswap v3, otherwise known as a single auto rebalancing range order. Smart Collateral will have a similar way of building up liquidity, through earning trading fees.





# Smart Debt

Smart Debt is a groundbreaking concept, marking the first time debt can be transformed into a productive asset by using it as liquidity for the DEX. Trades get routed through the Smart Debt liquidity and the trading fees are used to pay down the debt.

<figure><img src="https://1779047404-files.gitbook.io/~/files/v0/b/gitbook-x-prod.appspot.com/o/spaces%2F1GnplQv2H5lIIg0ygng0%2Fuploads%2FkFUibW5iXamIAmTDayLj%2FSmart-Debt-(1).gif?alt=media&#x26;token=340dd4b9-1713-4be0-982d-b5b2cf6d32c9" alt=""><figcaption><p>Users can now benefit from their debt being used as liquidity with the resulting fees paying down their debt.</p></figcaption></figure>

Smart Debt can be considered as the inverse of any AMM pool, utilizing the debt as liquidity rather than a token. While traditionally debt leaves the lending protocol, Smart Debt is still used as DEX trading liquidity.

#### Liquidity

Liquidity gets built up in the Smart Debt pool from users who borrow from a Smart Debt pool via any enabled vault. If a user deposits ETH collateral and takes on a USDC/USDT Smart Debt position, the borrowing is occurring through the Smart Debt pool and a share of that overall pool is allocated to that specific user.

#### Best Borrow Rates

Borrow rates will generally remain fairly stable, ranging from 3-5% depending on how the rate curve is being managed. The trading APR will vary from day to day, depending on the level of trading activity.&#x20;

This means that users will be able to get the best borrow rates in the industry due to this structure, even having the possibility of getting paid to borrow! This positions Fluid as the perfect place to manage your assets.

# Risks

Because of Fluid’s Liquidity Layer sourcing the same liquidity for the DEX and Lending, there is no risk of a liquidity crunch during either a market crash or surge.

### Market Crash Scenario

In the event of a sudden market crash, the market is selling crypto (for ex. ETH) for USDC. For the DEX protocol, this means that LPs will take ETH and give USDC back to the traders. This increases the ETH liquidity in the protocol, increasing the overall ETH liquidity in Fluid via the liquidity layer; thus doing the opposite of a liquidity crunch.

<figure><img src="https://1779047404-files.gitbook.io/~/files/v0/b/gitbook-x-prod.appspot.com/o/spaces%2F1GnplQv2H5lIIg0ygng0%2Fuploads%2FBkhqP0eIhw5OWrAW6U1E%2FRisk-Video-(1).gif?alt=media&#x26;token=487a151b-bd7c-4376-8261-ff24ef70a38d" alt=""><figcaption><p>Visual representation of how the DEX deals with risky scenarios.</p></figcaption></figure>

If we explain this in another way using the WBTC/ETH pairing, ETH is historically more volatile than BTC. So when there is an ongoing market crash, many participants are selling ETH for BTC. This also increases ETH liquidity in Fluid.&#x20;

The same principle applies to other pairs, such as stETH or eETH, which are paired with ETH. Fluid’s liquidity remains sufficient enough to handle liquidations, even in a market collapse. This is due to its advanced liquidation process, which is a 100x improvement over existing borrowing and lending protocols.

### Market Surge Scenario

During an upward market trend, the reverse happens. Liquidations shift to pairs where USDC is the collateral and ETH is the debt. As the price of ETH rises, more USDC flows into Fluid, while ETH exits the protocol.

This boosts USDC liquidity, ensuring smooth liquidations during market rallies.




# Protocol Details

### Vaults:

Vaults are composed of collateral and debt, in the DEX’s case, these Vaults can have 3 different appearances:

* Smart Collateral/Normal Debt: WBTC - cbBTC / USDC or weETH - ETH / wstETH
* Normal Collateral/Smart Debt: ETH / USDC - USDT or wstETH / USDC - USDT
* Smart Collateral/Smart Debt: WBTC - cbBTC / WBTC - cbBTC or wstETH - ETH / wstETH - ETH

**NFT** - Each position in a Vault is represented by an NFT. The NFT can be moved freely if needed. Each NFT comprises the Vault including its debt and collateral assets.

### Position Parameters:

**Net APY** - The Net APY earning from the Vault (supply interest - borrow interest).

**Collateral Factor** - Represents what percentage of the collateral can be borrowed against. Sometimes called LTV (Loan to Value).

**Liquidation Threshold** - The ratio at which the position would be considered under-collateralized and could be liquidated.

**Liquidation Penalty** - This is the penalty on your collateral asset for liquidating the position that has crossed the liquidation threshold, just until the threshold.

**Liquidation Price** - The collateral price at which the position would be considered under-collateralized and could be liquidated. Generally represented as liquidation price/current price of collateral.

**Net APR (Smart Collateral)** - Trading APR + Collateral tokens’ Net APR

**Net APR (Smart Debt)** - Debt tokens’ Net APR - Trading APR

### Vault Details:

**7d Volume** - The average volume of the last 7 days

**7d Fees** - The average fees earned by the pair in the last 7 days

**Trading Fees** - The fees that the pair is earning on the smart collateral/smart debt Vaults

**Revenue Cut** - The % of the fee that is sent to Fluid’s treasury

**Pool Price** - The exchange rate of wstETH to ETH determined by the amount of ETH / wstETH of wstETH in the pool

**Shift Time** - The time taken to complete the rebalancing

**AMM Curve** - The DEX uses the x\*y=k curve, but the liquidity is contracted within a range that is set by governance upon pool creation

**Lower Range** - Lower bound of the range that the Pool liquidity stays in

**Center Price** - The price at which the Pool rebalances itself once the Upper or Lower bounds are breached by the pool price

**Upper Range** - Upper bound of the range that the Pool liquidity stays in

**Lower Range (%)** - The momentary % difference that the Pool price is away from the Lower bound

**Upper Range (%)** - The momentary % difference that the Pool price is away from the Upper bound

**Lower Bound Price** - The lower threshold that, when breached, triggers the rebalancing

**Upper Bound Price** - The upper threshold that, when breached, triggers the rebalancing

**Center Min Price** - The minimum price up to, which the center price can move.

**Center Max Price** - The maximum price up to, which the center price can move.&#x20;

### Smart Debt:

**Total Debt** - Total amount of asset debt taken from the pool

**Effective Reserves** - The amount of the asset on the Liquidity Layer available to be taken as debt

#### Borrow Limit:

**Base Limit** - The minimum limit for a Vault's borrowing. The further expansion happens on this base.

**Current Limit** - The limit until where you can borrow

**Max Limit** - Maximum limit for a vault above which it is not possible to borrow

**Expand Percentage** - The rate at which Limits would increase or decrease over the given duration

**Expand Duration** - The time for which the limits expand at the given rate

**Borrowable** - Amount available for instant borrow

#### Smart Debt:

**Total Collateral** - Total amount of asset collateral put into the pool

**Effective Reserves** - The amount of the asset on the Liquidity Layer available to be taken as debt&#x20;

#### Withdrawal Limit:

**Base Limit** - The minimum limit for a Vault's withdrawal. The further expansion happens on this base

**Current Limit** - The limit until where you can withdraw. If it is $0, that means 100% of the users can withdraw

**Expand Percentage** - The rate at which Limits would increase or decrease over the given duration

**Expand Duration** - The time for which the limits expand at the given rate

**Withdrawable** - Amount available for instant withdrawal

**Withdrawal Gap** - Safety non-withdrawable amount to guarantee liquidations




# Dynamic Trading Fees

### Introduction

Dynamic trading fees were introduced to optimize trading volume while maintaining sustainable rewards for Smart Vault participants, following the execution of [IGP#84](https://atlas.instadapp.io/proposals/84).

The Dynamic trading fees are also applied on wstETH-ETH(#1) and USDC-ETH(#12) after the execution of [IGP#89](https://fluid.io/gov/proposals/89).

### Background

The USDC-USDT DEX Pool (#2) faced volume challenges that required a strategic solution. After careful analysis, we identified two potential approaches:

1. **Flat Fee Reduction**: Lower the fee to a static 0.003%
2. **Dynamic Fee Structure**: Implement a variable fee system based on price deviation

Similarly the dynamic fees have also been applied for wstETH-ETH(#1) and USDC-ETH(#12) in order to benefit the smart collateral and smart debt vault users with most optimized trading APR by micromanaging the trading fees.

### How Dynamic Fees Work&#x20;

The dynamic fee system uses a mathematical function based on the 3x² - 2x³ curve, which provides a smooth, responsive fee adjustment based on price deviation from peg.

#### Core Mechanics

* **Fee Range**: Fees dynamically adjust between 0.003% (minimum) and 0.01% (maximum)
* **Price Deviation**: Fees scale based on how far the USDC/USDT pool price deviates from 1.0
* **Mathematical Model**: The 3x² - 2x³ curve ensures fees increase proportionally to market volatility

#### Visual Representation

The fee structure can be visualized with:

* **Y-axis**: Trading fees (ranging from 0.003% to 0.01%)
* **X-axis**: Price deviation from 1.0 (e.g., 0.4% deviation means current price is 1.004)

<figure><img src="https://1779047404-files.gitbook.io/~/files/v0/b/gitbook-x-prod.appspot.com/o/spaces%2F1GnplQv2H5lIIg0ygng0%2Fuploads%2FZ3sVwOyf7X8Tgm6D74q6%2Fimage.png?alt=media&#x26;token=8ce15094-9bdc-4237-ae69-73ac809d4922" alt=""><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

#### Implementation Example

| Price Deviation | Applied Fee |
| --------------- | ----------- |
| 0.000% (at peg) | 0.003%      |
| 0.100%          | \~0.004%    |
| 0.200%          | \~0.006%    |
| 0.300%          | \~0.008%    |
| 0.400%+         | 0.010%      |

### Benefits of Dynamic Fees

This implementation offers several advantages:

1. **Enhanced Market Efficiency**: Lower fees during stable market conditions encourage trading and arbitrage resulting in higher volumes
2. **Volatility Protection**: Higher fees during price deviations restricts the change of pool price and makes it stay in range
3. **Balanced Incentives**: Optimizes between trading volume and tradingAPR

### Future Considerations

The protocol governance continues to monitor this implementation and may propose adjustments based on:

* Market conditions
* Competitive landscape
* Feedback from traders and liquidity providers
